How Raleigh Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many early failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Drain and flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually includes the month and year it was built, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that point and also shows discolored water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a flooded utility room.
