A tankless water heater takes a different approach: instead of keeping 40 or 50 gallons hot around the clock, it heats water only while a tap is open. Flow through the unit trips a sensor, a powerful burner or electric element fires, and the water is heated as it passes through a compact heat exchanger. The upside is significant: no standby heat loss, hot water that does not run out during back-to-back showers, a wall-mounted unit that frees up floor space, and a service life that often reaches twenty years with maintenance. For plenty of Chapel Hill, NC households, that makes tankless a smart upgrade.

A good tankless install starts with math, not tools. We total your peak demand in gallons per minute, such as a shower, a kitchen sink, and the dishwasher at once, and compare it against the temperature rise your incoming water needs in winter. That tells us the real capacity you need. Next we check the supply side. Gas tankless units fire at very high input, so a half-inch line that fed an old tank is often too small. Condensing models vent through PVC or polypropylene and need a condensate drain, while non-condensing units need stainless steel venting. Electric models are their own case, often needing multiple large breakers and sometimes a service upgrade.

When we install a tankless heater, we build it to be serviced. That means isolation valves for descaling, a gas line with a sediment trap sized for the burner's full demand, venting that meets the manufacturer's rules for length and termination, and a condensate line with a neutralizer on condensing models. Where water hardness is high, we explain scale protection options before the unit goes on the wall. Optional recirculation can shorten the wait at distant fixtures. We handle permits where required, test the unit at full flow, and show you how it works. Homeowners in Chapel Hill can call (984) 273-8652 for a demand assessment and a clear quote.

How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Construction or remodeling that opens walls and simplifies the install
  • Preferring a heater that can be repaired and maintained instead of replaced every decade
  • A tank water heater that is ten years old or more and due for replacement
  • A utility area where a bulky tank takes up room you would rather use
  • Paying to keep a full tank hot even when no one is home
  • Adding a bathroom, soaking tub, or larger shower that needs more hot water
  • An addition or guest suite that is a long run from the main heater
  • Showers turning lukewarm before everyone gets a turn

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Buying the right unit is the easy part. The other half is making sure your home can support it. Gas tankless burners can fire at close to 199,000 BTU, so the size of the gas line, the capacity of the meter, and the pressure at high fire all have to check out. Vent materials, lengths, and terminations are dictated by the manufacturer and local code, and condensing units add a condensate drain that should be neutralized. Electric whole-home units can pull more current than an older panel can deliver. Get any of these wrong, and the unit underperforms or becomes unsafe.

A professional install protects both performance and safety. We start with a real demand assessment, confirm the gas and electrical supply, and design venting that meets the manufacturer's requirements. The install includes service valves, a sediment trap, a correctly routed relief valve line, and a neutralized condensate drain where needed, followed by a full-flow test. Just as important, we tell you plainly if a tank, hybrid, or smaller point-of-use unit would serve you better. The goal is the right water heater, not just a tankless sale.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here are our recommendations:

  • Do not cut power to a unit in a cold space, since its freeze protection needs electricity
  • Keep the inlet screen clear so flow stays strong and the unit fires reliably
  • Plan on yearly descaling through the service valves, sooner if your water is hard
  • If the unit shows a recurring code, note it and have it checked
  • Hard water shortens tankless life, so ask about scale protection
  • Set the temperature around 120F for safety and efficiency
  • Check the vent cap outside for nests, debris, or buildup
