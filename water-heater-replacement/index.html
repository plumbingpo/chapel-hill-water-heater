Most tank water heaters in Chapel Hill, NC last somewhere between eight and twelve years, and hard water, high pressure, and skipped maintenance can shorten that. The end usually announces itself in one of a few ways: a slow leak from the bottom of the tank, rusty hot water, rumbling that grows louder every month, or a supply that runs out sooner each winter. Nothing is clearer than a leak from the tank body. Once the glass lining fails and the steel rusts through, no part can fix it, and a tank that is weeping today can let go without warning tomorrow.

A new water heater should fit the way your household uses hot water today, not the way it did when the old one went in. Warning Signs to Watch For

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Hot water running out faster every month
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank
  • A tank at or past the eight-to-twelve-year mark

Don't Wait for a Total Failure

A little rust-colored water or a relief valve that drips now and then can be the first hint of a tank that is close to the end. If you notice any of these signs, contact Chapel Hill Water Heater at (984) 273-8652 for a prompt inspection.

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Replacement

Swapping a water heater looks simple until you count the connections. A gas unit needs a leak-free gas connection with a sediment trap, a vent that is sized and pitched correctly so carbon monoxide goes outside, and combustion air it can actually reach. Electric units need a breaker and circuit that match the element wattage. Every tank needs a correctly rated relief valve with a discharge pipe routed to a safe spot, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code. Going tankless or switching to a heat pump adds even more: bigger gas lines, different vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

A professional replacement handles every one of those details once. Our experienced technicians size the heater to your household, verify the gas line, vent, and electrical service, and install whatever code items apply. Before leaving, we leak-test, verify draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and set a safe temperature. It is what separates a heater that runs quietly for years from one that has problems from the start. And when a repair would give your current tank several more good years, we say so instead of selling you a new one.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
  • Plan ahead: knowing the heater's age lets you replace it on your schedule, not after a leak
  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Descale a tankless unit every year, or more often with hard water
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
